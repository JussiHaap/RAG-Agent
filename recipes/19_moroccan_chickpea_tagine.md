MOROCCAN-INSPIRED CHICKPEA AND APRICOT TAGINE

ABOUT THIS RECIPE
A warming one-pot dish with chickpeas, carrots, tomatoes, dried apricots, and fragrant spices. It is hearty enough for a vegetarian main course and works well for batch cooking. The flavor is sweet-savory rather than hot; add chili if you prefer more heat.

Serves: 4
Preparation: 20 minutes
Cooking: 45–50 minutes
Total: Approximately 1 hour 10 minutes
Difficulty: Easy to moderate

INGREDIENTS

Vegetables and legumes:
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 2 carrots, cut into thick half-moons
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 cans chickpeas, about 480 g drained weight total, rinsed
- 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
- 300 ml vegetable stock
- 100 g dried apricots, roughly chopped

Spices:
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp sweet paprika
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp chili flakes, optional
- Salt and black pepper

To finish:
- Zest and juice of 1/2 lemon
- 30 g toasted almonds, roughly chopped
- Fresh coriander or parsley
- Plain yogurt, optional

To serve:
- Couscous, rice, or flatbread

METHOD

1. **Prepare everything.** Chop the onion, carrots, pepper, garlic, and apricots. Drain and rinse the chickpeas. Measure the spices into a small bowl so they can be added without delaying the cooking.
2. **Soften the onion.** Heat the olive oil in a large heavy saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 5–7 minutes until soft and translucent.
3. **Add vegetables.** Stir in carrots and bell pepper and cook for another 4 minutes. Add garlic and cook for 30 seconds.
4. **Toast the spices.** Add cumin, coriander, paprika, cinnamon, and optional chili flakes. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant, taking care not to scorch them.
5. **Add liquids.** Pour in tomatoes and vegetable stock. Stir and scrape the bottom of the pan to release browned bits.
6. **Add chickpeas and apricots.** Stir them in and bring the mixture to a gentle simmer. Reduce the heat to low and partially cover.
7. **Simmer.** Cook for 30–35 minutes, stirring every 8–10 minutes. The carrots should be tender and the sauce thickened. If the pan becomes too dry, add 50–100 ml water or stock. If too thin at the end, simmer uncovered.
8. **Balance the flavor.** Stir in lemon zest and half the lemon juice. Taste before adding more. Season with salt and black pepper.
9. **Toast almonds.** Heat a dry frying pan over medium-low heat. Toast almonds for 2–3 minutes, shaking frequently, until lightly golden and fragrant.
10. **Serve.** Spoon the tagine over couscous or rice, or serve with warm flatbread. Sprinkle with almonds and fresh herbs. Add yogurt if desired.

HOW TO MAKE COUSCOUS
Put 250 g couscous in a heatproof bowl with a pinch of salt and one teaspoon of olive oil. Pour over 300 ml boiling water or hot vegetable stock. Cover for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. Follow the package instructions if the brand specifies a different liquid ratio.

VARIATIONS
- Add 150 g diced sweet potato with the carrots.
- Replace apricots with raisins or chopped dates.
- Add spinach during the final 2 minutes.
- Serve with roasted cauliflower or grilled halloumi on the side.

MAKE-AHEAD AND STORAGE
The tagine can be cooked a day ahead and refrigerated in a sealed container for up to 3 days. Reheat gently, adding a splash of water if necessary. It freezes well for up to 3 months. Add toasted almonds and fresh herbs after reheating.

NOTES
The dish thickens as it cools because chickpeas and vegetables continue to absorb liquid. Adjust consistency when reheating. For a gluten-free meal, serve with rice instead of couscous or wheat-based flatbread.
